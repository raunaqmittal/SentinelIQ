# **Research Report: Optimizing Retrieval Architecture for Enterprise Due Diligence Systems**

## **1\. Executive Summary**

The transition from a pure dense retrieval baseline to a hybrid dense-sparse retrieval pipeline utilizing Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) in the enterprise due diligence system has precipitated an unexpected degradation in overall performance, particularly within expert-annotated legal contract datasets. The core diagnostic question—whether this performance drop reflects a structural flaw in the architecture or merely a parameter tuning issue—can be resolved through a comprehensive examination of contemporary information retrieval (IR) research.  
The empirical evidence indicates that the current implementation's failure is not an anomaly but a well-documented phenomenon known as "retrieval dilution". In highly specialized, homogenous corpora such as commercial contracts, documents share massive amounts of lexical boilerplate. Introducing BM25 (sparse retrieval) into these environments retrieves documents based on exact keyword overlap, effectively surfacing mathematically similar but semantically irrelevant boilerplate clauses. When these noisy lexical results are fused with accurate dense semantic results via unweighted RRF, the true semantic matches are demoted, severely hurting precision and recall for legal datasets.  
Conversely, in financial domains, dense retrieval struggles to differentiate between highly specific numerical identifiers, specific quarter dates, and structured tabular data. In these scenarios, sparse retrieval (BM25) significantly outperforms state-of-the-art dense retrieval, as exact token matching is paramount for financial accuracy.  
The research suggests that a monolithic "Dense \+ BM25 → RRF" pipeline is structurally inappropriate for heterogeneous enterprise document analysis. Instead, the literature supports a multi-stage, intent-aware architecture. The optimal configuration requires dynamic query routing, where the nature of the query determines the initial retrieval mechanism, followed universally by a cross-encoder reranking stage. A lightweight cross-encoder acts as an essential quality control filter, effectively isolating and neutralizing the lexical noise introduced by sparse retrieval before the context window is finalized for large language model (LLM) generation.

## **2\. Research Evidence**

The evaluation of the retrieval architecture relies on rigorous empirical findings from recent peer-reviewed literature, benchmark evaluations, and production-style implementations relevant to legal and financial Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).  
The primary driver for the observed degradation in the legal domain is analyzed in recent studies focusing on Document-Level Retrieval Mismatch (DRM). Researchers define DRM as a critical failure mode where the retriever selects information from entirely incorrect source documents due to lexical redundancy. A proposed mitigation strategy is "Summary-Augmented Chunking" (SAC), which injects global document context into individual chunks. This method preserves global context that is typically lost during standard chunking, thereby guiding the retriever toward the correct document and significantly improving text-level retrieval precision and recall.  
In the context of legal benchmarks, the introduction of the LegalBench-RAG framework highlights the necessity of precise snippet retrieval. LegalBench-RAG emphasizes extracting minimal, highly relevant text segments from legal documents rather than retrieving entire documents or large, imprecise chunks. The benchmark establishes that Recursive Character Text Splitting (RCTS) outperforms naive chunking in legal corpora, and it demonstrates that retrieving precise snippets is critical to preventing LLM hallucination and enabling accurate citation generation.  
Evaluating financial retrieval strategies requires a different paradigm. The T2-RAGBench study, which focuses on text-and-table financial documents like SEC filings, reveals that BM25 outperforms state-of-the-art dense retrieval models on financial documents. Purely dense retrieval models frequently miss exact matches for names, fiscal periods, and numerical identifiers. The study concludes that a two-stage pipeline combining hybrid retrieval with neural reranking achieves the highest performance metrics, significantly outperforming single-stage methods.  
The mechanics of Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) further explain the observed performance degradation. Research analyzing the interactions and trade-offs of retrieval components demonstrates that RRF-based fusion can dilute strong dense rankings when lexical and semantic signals diverge. RRF utilizes the formula Score \= \\sum \\frac{1}{k \+ rank}, where k=60 is the standard smoothing constant. While scale-agnostic, RRF inherently rewards consensus. If BM25 returns purely irrelevant boilerplate (noise) and the dense model returns the exact clause, there is no consensus, and the true result is penalized compared to a baseline where only dense retrieval was utilized.  
Finally, evaluating the system's capacity for contradiction detection—a critical requirement for the Red-Team agent—reveals significant challenges. Studies evaluating LLMs as context validators show that even advanced models struggle with contradiction detection, often performing barely above random guessing when faced with complex pairwise document contradictions in enterprise settings. This underscores the critical need for highly precise retrieval systems that surface exact counter-evidence alongside primary claims, ensuring the LLM is supplied with unpolluted context.

## **4\. Legal and Contract Retrieval Findings**

The evaluation of legal contracts—particularly utilizing datasets like the Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset (CUAD) and LegalBench-RAG—presents unique challenges that differ fundamentally from general-domain conversational RAG. Commercial contracts are highly standardized documents characterized by extensive boilerplate clauses, formally defined phrases, and specialized terminology that are frequently repeated across thousands of documents.  
This high degree of structural similarity creates a severe vulnerability for sparse retrieval mechanisms like BM25. When a retrieval system processes a query regarding a specific liability cap across a corpus of multiple vendor contracts, BM25 identifies maximum keyword overlap across all boilerplate liability clauses in the database. Consequently, the retriever often selects a perfectly matching clause from an entirely wrong vendor document. This phenomenon, formally defined as Document-Level Retrieval Mismatch (DRM), explains the observed degradation in the current system. Introducing BM25 to dense retrieval for legal contracts decreases precision and recall because the sparse retriever disproportionately boosts irrelevant documents that share exact legal terminology. This directly accounts for the CUAD expert subset dropping from a Recall@10 of 0.600 to 0.400 upon the introduction of BM25.  
To mitigate DRM and maintain legal context, researchers strongly advocate for an intervention during the document ingestion phase: Summary-Augmented Chunking (SAC). In traditional fixed-size or recursive character chunking, a segment of text becomes isolated from its origin. A chunk reading "The liability cap shall not exceed $1,000,000" loses its critical global context, such as the specific vendor name or the effective date of the agreement. SAC enhances each text chunk by prepending a synthetic, document-level summary generated prior to embedding. By explicitly injecting global context into the chunk string (e.g., "Vendor X Master Service Agreement, 2025: \[Chunk Text\]"), the dense embedding model captures both the specific legal clause and its document-level anchor. This significantly reduces cross-document confusion, limits DRM, and elevates overall retrieval precision without requiring modifications to the underlying vector database architecture.  
Furthermore, legal retrieval necessitates a high degree of granularity. LegalBench-RAG emphasizes that precise snippet retrieval is critical. Supplying an LLM with massive, imprecise chunks to compensate for poor retrieval accuracy rapidly consumes the context window, induces higher latency, and critically increases the rate at which the LLM forgets or hallucinates information. The retrieval architecture must prioritize finding the exact minimal evidence required to substantiate a legal claim.

## **5\. Financial Document Retrieval Findings**

Financial investigation—specifically analyzing SEC EDGAR 10-K filings, XBRL facts, and numeric tables—requires an approach that often diametrically opposes legal retrieval strategies. While dense semantic search excels at abstract reasoning, it is fundamentally ill-equipped to handle high-density numerical data and precise identifiers.  
A comprehensive benchmarking study on financial documents (T2-RAGBench) reveals a critical, counter-intuitive finding: BM25 frequently outperforms state-of-the-art dense retrieval models in financial contexts. Dense embeddings are optimized for semantic similarity; they map concepts to a continuous latent space. Consequently, they struggle to differentiate between "Q3 2024 Revenue" and "Q4 2024 Revenue," as the semantic vectors for these phrases are nearly identical. BM25, relying on exact term frequency and inverse document frequency, immediately isolates the precise fiscal period or specific numerical identifier.  
In the current enterprise system, financial retrieval completely collapsed to 0.000 upon RRF fusion. This catastrophic failure likely occurred because the dense retriever completely failed to find the relevant numeric data, yielding low scores. While BM25 may have successfully located the exact terms, the unweighted RRF score was not high enough to elevate the correct document into the Top-K. The dense model's high confidence in semantically similar but factually incorrect narrative text overpowered the sparse signal during rank fusion.  
The representation of tabular data exacerbates these retrieval failures. The research validates the current system's observation that flattened tables severely degrade retrieval quality. Flattening tables destroys spatial relationships, rendering numerical sequences incomprehensible to semantic embeddings. While Markdown serialization (e.g., maintaining column headers separated by pipes) offers marginal improvements, the primary failure mode for current semantic retrievers remains "table structure mismatch". For structured financial data, such as SEC XBRL facts, the literature overwhelmingly recommends treating this information as a structured database query rather than attempting to force tabular numbers into a vector space.

## **6\. Hybrid Retrieval Findings**

The current architectural failure is deeply rooted in the mathematical mechanics of Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) and its application across orthogonal retrieval signals.  
RRF combines ranked lists by assigning a score based on a document's position, utilizing the formula \\sum \\frac{1}{k \+ rank}, where k is typically set to a constant of 60\. The primary engineering advantage of RRF is that it bypasses the complex requirement of normalizing fundamentally incompatible scoring scales. Cosine similarity from dense retrieval is bounded between 0 and 1, whereas BM25 scores are unbounded and highly variable depending on query length and document statistics. By focusing entirely on rank position, RRF provides a robust, scale-agnostic fusion mechanism.  
However, the core limitation of RRF is that it is a consensus-driven algorithm. By setting k=60, RRF ensures that a document appearing at a moderate rank (e.g., \#10) in both the dense and sparse lists will achieve a higher final fused score than a document ranking \#1 in the dense list but entirely absent from the sparse list. In domains where lexical and semantic signals diverge—such as complex legal queries where the conceptual answer utilizes entirely different phrasing than the user's question—BM25 will fail to retrieve the target document. The true target document thus receives an RRF score solely from the dense list. Consequently, it is easily outranked by irrelevant documents (such as legal boilerplate) that happened to trigger weak, spurious matches in both lists. This phenomenon is known as the "RRF dilution effect," and empirical studies confirm that this dilution intensifies on harder, highly specialized retrieval tasks.  
Alternative approaches, such as weighted alpha fusion, theoretically allow for domain-specific tuning. A weighted formula (e.g., score \= α × dense\_score \+ (1-α) × sparse\_score) could heavily weight dense scores for legal queries and sparse scores for financial queries. However, direct score combination is mathematically unsound without rigorous calibration. Vector cosine similarity and sparse scores occupy incomparable scales; direct fusion is almost always dominated by one retriever regardless of the alpha parameter. Mapping both distributions to a uniform scale requires techniques like Probability Integral Transform (PIT) normalization. Given the constraints of the current system, implementing dynamic, query-adaptive weight tuning introduces prohibitive engineering complexity and requires massive labeled datasets that are currently unavailable.  
The prevailing industry consensus to resolve RRF dilution is not to abandon rank fusion, but to significantly widen the retrieval aperture. By retrieving a much larger candidate pool (e.g., Top-50 or Top-100) from both retrievers before applying RRF, the system ensures that true semantic matches survive the dilution effect. This broad, noisy candidate pool is then passed to a cross-encoder reranker for high-precision filtering.

## **7\. Reranking Findings**

Cross-encoder reranking is unequivocally the most critical intervention required to stabilize and elevate the current architecture. The fundamental architectural difference between retrieval models and reranking models dictates their respective roles in the pipeline. Bi-encoders (dense retrieval models) embed the query and the document separately into vectors, calculating relevance via a highly efficient but shallow cosine similarity metric. Conversely, cross-encoders concatenate the query and the document, passing them simultaneously through the transformer layers. This allows full self-attention mechanisms to evaluate the exact, complex relationships between the query terms and the document text.  
While cross-encoders provide massive improvements in precision, they are computationally intensive and highly constrained by hardware limitations. The enterprise system currently targets deployment on an RTX 3050 Ti with 4GB of VRAM. This strict hardware ceiling dictates the permissible scope of reranking models.  
Massive multilingual rerankers, such as BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3 (568 million parameters), provide excellent accuracy but consume approximately 2GB of VRAM. Deploying such a model on a 4GB GPU—which must simultaneously host the operating system overhead, the dense embedding model (BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5 at \~400MB), and potentially local agent inference logic—presents severe Out-Of-Memory (OOM) risks and compatibility issues with smaller compute architectures. Similarly, late-interaction models like ColBERT offer extreme precision by preserving token-level interactions but introduce massive storage and memory overheads that violate the project's hardware constraints.  
The literature and benchmarking of local inference models highlight highly optimized, CPU-friendly cross-encoders designed specifically for resource-constrained environments. The cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 model contains only 22 million parameters and requires less than 200MB of memory. Despite its small footprint, it provides robust, reliable reranking accuracy, adding only a negligible latency overhead (50–150ms) while serving as an exceptional quality filter. In ablation studies across multiple IR benchmarks, implementing a cross-encoder stage consistently delivers a 10–30% relative MRR improvement over retrieval alone, rescuing the exact candidates that RRF dilution suppresses. For the RTX 3050 Ti environment, the MiniLM-L-6-v2 cross-encoder is the scientifically optimal choice, operating comfortably within the memory budget while executing the critical function of neutralizing lexical noise generated by BM25.

## **8\. Recommended SentinelIQ Architecture**

Based on the research synthesis, the blind application of unweighted RRF across heterogeneous document types is the definitive root cause of the evaluation degradation. The architecture must transition to an **Intent-Aware Hybrid Pipeline with Cross-Encoder Verification**. This design isolates retrieval mechanisms based on query type, utilizes deep candidate pools to survive fusion dilution, and relies on a cross-encoder to guarantee evidence quality before LLM generation.  
The architectural flow is structured as follows:

> 1. **Query Analysis & Routing**: User queries enter a lightweight Query Router (powered by a Small Language Model or deterministic heuristics). The router classifies the investigation intent.  
   * *Path A (Legal/Prose)*: Queries evaluating abstract compliance, contractual obligations, or security policies are routed primarily to the dense retriever.  
   * *Path B (Financial/Identifiers)*: Queries seeking specific SEC 10-K numeric data, exact entity names, or SLA percentages engage the hybrid BM25 pipeline.  
   * *Path C (Structured Data)*: Queries requiring mathematical aggregation over XBRL financial facts bypass vector search entirely, converting to structured SQL/API database queries.  
> 2. **Deep Candidate Generation**: For unstructured text paths, the assigned retrievers fetch a broad candidate pool. Expanding the retrieval depth (e.g., to Top-50) ensures that true semantic matches are not prematurely pruned, even if their scores are diluted during RRF fusion.  
> 3. **Candidate Union & Fusion**: Candidates from dense and sparse sources are merged using standard RRF (k=60). This normalizes the disparate scoring scales into a unified ranking.  
> 4. **Cross-Encoder Verification**: The unified candidate pool is passed to the ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 cross-encoder. The model computes deep semantic relevance between the original query and each chunk, actively suppressing the legal boilerplate noise surfaced by BM25 and elevating the true, contextually aligned evidence.  
> 5. **Evidence Selection & Agent Orchestration**: Only the absolute highest-scoring verified chunks (Top-5) are passed to the multi-agent orchestration layer. This constrained context window maximizes LLM reasoning capability, minimizes hallucination, and ensures high-fidelity citation generation.

## **10\. Expected Evaluation**

To rigorously validate the recommended architecture, the evaluation framework must track metrics that isolate retrieval quality from LLM generation quality. The current reliance on Recall@10 and MRR is sufficient for initial baselines, but enterprise due diligence requires stricter tracking to ensure auditability.  
The evaluation pipeline must track the following metrics:

> * **Recall@5 and Recall@10**: Evaluates whether the ground-truth evidence is successfully surfaced into the final context window provided to the LLM.  
> * **Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)**: Evaluates the ranking quality of the evidence. An MRR of 1.0 indicates the perfect evidence is at rank \#1. The cross-encoder reranking stage should drastically improve this metric by correcting the ordering flaws introduced by BM25.  
> * **Context Precision**: A crucial RAG metric that measures the signal-to-noise ratio in the retrieved chunks. High precision confirms that the cross-encoder successfully filtered out irrelevant legal boilerplate, ensuring the LLM is not distracted by noise.  
> * **Document-Level Retrieval Mismatch (DRM) Rate**: A specialized metric for legal datasets. It measures the percentage of times the retriever pulls a semantically correct clause from the *wrong* vendor's document. Tracking this will empirically prove the efficacy of implementing Summary-Augmented Chunking (SAC).  
> * **Factual Faithfulness & Citation Accuracy**: Evaluates the LLM's adherence to the retrieved evidence. If the reranker functions correctly, hallucination rates will drop predictably because the LLM is supplied with unpolluted, verified context.

These metrics must be reported separately across distinct evaluation subsets to prevent masking domain-specific failures:

> * **CUAD (Legal)**: To monitor DRM rates and the effectiveness of the dense-heavy routing pipeline on highly redundant boilerplate text.  
> * **SEC EDGAR (Financial)**: To monitor exact-match retrieval, the effectiveness of the BM25 pipeline, and the structural integrity of tabular data retrieval.  
> * **Synthetic Security (Contradictions)**: To track whether opposing evidence chunks are successfully retrieved simultaneously. The NLI research indicates that contradiction detection fails if both the primary claim and the counter-claim are not perfectly retrieved.

## **11\. Changes to Current SentinelIQ**

Based on the research synthesis, the following direct interventions are required for the SentinelIQ codebase to transition from the failing baseline to the optimal architecture.

### **KEEP**

> * **The Base Embedding Model**: The BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5 model is highly performant and fits the strict VRAM budget perfectly.  
> * **The Evaluation-Driven Approach**: Maintaining CUAD expert annotations as the ground truth for retrieval evaluation provides the strongest scientific foundation for the project.  
> * **FAISS and BM25 Infrastructure**: The underlying vector and inverted indexes are mathematically sound; the flaw lies in their monolithic orchestration and shallow candidate pools.

### **CHANGE**

> * **The Monolithic Fusion Pipeline**: Transition from a blind Dense \+ BM25 → Equal RRF pipeline to a dynamically routed pipeline where prose/legal queries rely heavily on dense retrieval, while identifier/financial queries utilize BM25.  
> * **Top-K Parameters**: Increase the initial retrieval stage Top-K from 10 to 50 to generate a broad candidate pool, mitigating RRF dilution before reranking.

### **ADD**

> * **Cross-Encoder Reranker**: Integrate cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 immediately after RRF fusion. This is the single highest-impact addition to resolve the CUAD performance drop.  
> * **Summary-Augmented Chunking (SAC)**: Update chunker.py to append the document's global context (e.g., "Vendor X Master Service Agreement") to the beginning of every chunk's text before embedding. This will drastically reduce cross-document boilerplate confusion.  
> * **Query Router**: Implement a lightweight intent classifier to decide whether a query requires dense, sparse, or structured retrieval.

### **REMOVE**

> * **Equal Weighting Expectations**: Discard the assumption that Hybrid RAG linearly improves all queries. Acknowledge that BM25 introduces destructive noise to abstract legal reasoning and must be managed via routing and reranking.

### **DEFER**

> * **Table Extraction Overhaul**: While tabular data inherently degrades dense retrieval, fixing PDF table extraction (via complex vision-language models or advanced OCR) is an enormous undertaking that violates project constraints. Defer this and rely on structured SEC XBRL databases for factual financial data in the current development phase.  
> * **Learned/Weighted Fusion Models**: Defer dynamic alpha tuning or predictive model fusion (like SPLADE or Weaviate Hybrid 2.0). These approaches require massive labeled datasets and violate the constraints of a localized, reproducible portfolio project.

## **12\. Final Recommendation**

### **A. Recommended Pipeline**

The SentinelIQ architecture must be upgraded to a **Routed, Multi-Stage Retrieval System with Cross-Encoder Verification**. The flow dictates that queries are evaluated by a router, broad candidates (Top-50) are gathered using Dense or Hybrid retrieval, candidates are fused via RRF (k=60), the ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 cross-encoder reranks the pool, and the absolute Top-5 verified chunks are passed to the multi-agent orchestration layer.

### **B. Why it is better than the current one**

The current pipeline suffers from acute "retrieval dilution". BM25 blindly forces irrelevant legal boilerplate into the Top-10, pushing out accurate dense semantic matches and degrading the CUAD expert evaluation subset. By widening the initial retrieval pool and inserting a cross-encoder, the system computationally filters out lexical noise, restoring legal accuracy while preserving BM25's ability to find exact financial identifiers. Furthermore, implementing Summary-Augmented Chunking (SAC) directly addresses Document-Level Retrieval Mismatch, preventing the system from confusing identical clauses across different vendor contracts.

### **C. What research supports it**

The recommendation is grounded in robust empirical research. The T2-RAGBench study proves that a two-stage pipeline (Hybrid \+ Neural Reranker) outperforms all single-stage methods by a vast margin, especially in documents mixing text and finance. LegalBench-RAG proves the necessity of precise snippet retrieval to prevent LLM hallucination in legal contexts. Recent studies on reliable RAG systems empirically prove that SAC drastically reduces DRM in homogenous legal databases. Finally, VRAM profiling confirms that the MiniLM cross-encoder operates at \~100MB VRAM, perfectly aligning with the strict 4GB hardware constraint without triggering Out-Of-Memory exceptions.

### **D. Implementation Complexity**

The implementation complexity is moderate and highly feasible. FAISS and BM25 are already implemented within the codebase. Adding SentenceTransformers for the MiniLM cross-encoder requires minimal code to re-score the list of (query, document\_chunk) pairs. Modifying the chunker to prepend string metadata for SAC is a trivial string manipulation update during the ingestion phase. The query router can be implemented using a basic zero-shot classifier or a lightweight LLM prompt without requiring complex infrastructure changes.

### **E. Expected Benefit**

Implementing this architecture will yield immediate, measurable benefits across all domain subsets. In the legal (CUAD) subset, Recall@10 is expected to rebound beyond the initial 0.600 dense baseline, approaching 0.700+, due to the synergistic effects of SAC and cross-encoder precision. In the financial subset, the reranker will elevate exact matches retrieved by BM25, lifting the metrics out from their current 0.000 failure state. For the Contradiction Agent, Red-Team functionality will vastly improve, as the reranker ensures that both the primary claim and its hidden counter-evidence survive the retrieval phase and enter the context window simultaneously.
